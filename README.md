# Blind Spot Mirror

> **See what your reasoning might be missing.**

## 1. Chosen Vertical

**Decision Support / Personal Productivity**

Blind Spot Mirror focuses on helping people make better-informed decisions by identifying gaps in their reasoning. It is designed for decisions where there may not be one objectively correct answer, such as career, education, financial, time-management, and personal choices.

The solution is intentionally **decision-supportive, not decision-making**: the AI helps users examine their thinking but never chooses an option for them.

---

## 2. Approach & Logic

The core idea is to turn AI from a **recommendation engine into a reasoning mirror**.

Instead of:

```text
User → AI → Recommendation
```

Blind Spot Mirror uses:

```text
User Reasoning
      ↓
AI Analysis
      ↓
Potential Blind Spots
      ↓
User Reflection
      ↓
Reasoning Comparison
      ↓
User Decision
```

The user's reasoning is analyzed across four dimensions:

* **Assumptions** — beliefs the user may be treating as facts
* **Missing Factors** — relevant considerations not yet addressed
* **Perspectives** — people, stakeholders, or viewpoints that may have been overlooked
* **Evidence Gaps** — claims or uncertainties that require further information

The AI then generates targeted questions that encourage the user to investigate these areas themselves.

---

## 3. How the Solution Works

### Step 1 — Frame

The user enters:

* The decision they are facing
* Their current reasoning
* Relevant context

For example:

> **Should I seriously prepare for GATE 2027 or prioritize campus placements?**

The user explains why they are considering each path and what concerns them.

---

### Step 2 — Mirror

Google's AI model analyzes the reasoning and produces structured potential blind spots.

The application organizes these into an interactive **Blind Spot Map** rather than presenting a generic chatbot response.

Example:

```text
             MY REASONING
                  │
      ┌───────────┼───────────┐
      ↓           ↓           ↓
 Assumptions  Missing      Evidence
              Factors       Gaps
                  │
             Perspectives
```

Each blind spot includes questions designed to help the user examine it.

---

### Step 3 — Reflect

The user works through the identified blind spots.

For each reflection, they can:

* Respond to the question
* Indicate their confidence
* Mark whether they have addressed the issue
* Identify areas where they still need information

The system tracks this process so the user can see which blind spots have been explored and which remain unresolved.

---

### Step 4 — Stress Test

The user revisits their original reasoning after reflection.

Blind Spot Mirror presents:

```text
BEFORE
What I originally thought

        ↓

REFLECTION
What I discovered or reconsidered

        ↓

NOW
How my reasoning has changed
```

The user can indicate whether they are now:

* More confident
* Less certain
* Still in need of information

---

### Step 5 — User-Owned Decision

The final decision is written by the **user**, not generated by the AI.

The application summarizes:

* What the user originally focused on
* What they explored
* What remains uncertain
* Questions worth investigating further

The user then records their own final reasoning and decision.

---

## 4. AI Logic

The AI is used specifically for **reasoning analysis and question generation**.

It is instructed not to:

* Make the decision for the user
* Recommend one option as the "best"
* Present assumptions as facts
* Pretend uncertainty does not exist

Instead, it should surface possibilities such as:

> "You may be assuming these two options are mutually exclusive."

or:

> "What evidence would help you determine whether this assumption is true?"

This keeps the human in control while still making AI useful in the decision-making process.

---

## 5. Assumptions Made

The solution makes the following assumptions:

1. **Users can describe their current reasoning in text.**
   The quality of blind-spot detection depends partly on the information provided by the user.

2. **Not every identified blind spot is necessarily a real problem.**
   AI-generated blind spots are treated as prompts for investigation, not as facts.

3. **Some decisions cannot and should not have an automated answer.**
   Personal priorities, values, constraints, and uncertainty can make a decision highly individual.

4. **Reflection is more valuable when it is interactive.**
   Users are therefore asked to respond to questions rather than simply receiving an AI-generated analysis.

5. **More information does not automatically mean a better decision.**
   The goal is to improve the user's reasoning process, not overwhelm them with information.

6. **The final decision belongs to the user.**
   Blind Spot Mirror provides structured reflection and does not replace human judgment.

---

## 6. Core Principle

> **The AI holds up the mirror. The user makes the decision.**

Blind Spot Mirror is built around a simple idea: AI does not always need to give us a better answer. Sometimes, it needs to help us ask **better questions about how we arrived at our answer**.
